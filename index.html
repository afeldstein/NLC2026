Beyond the Productivity Trap

Reimagining Agentic AI as Actor in Networked Learning
NLC 2026 Roundtable — Andrew Feldstein, D.P.S. — Fort Hays State University

Card 1 Disruption
The Productivity Trade-Off
"AI makes teams faster. It also makes them quieter. If the silence is where trust, negotiation, and mutual understanding used to live, what did we gain?"
The Finding
In a large-scale experiment with 2,310 participants, human-AI teams produced 50% more output per worker. But human-human teams engaged in 29% more social and emotional communication — greetings, encouragement, rapport-building, self-disclosure.
Ju & Aral (2025)
Card 2 Disruption
The Network Collapse
"The internet let us build our own networks instead of relying on the ones that formed naturally through proximity. AI lets us skip the networks entirely. No one needed to ask a classmate."
The Finding
In five of six collaborative groups with a GAI chatbot, students interacted significantly more with the chatbot than with peers — individually, not as a shared resource. The chatbot became the dominant network hub.
Feng (2025)
Card 3 Disruption
The Satisfaction Paradox
"The students who used AI chatbots the most and liked them the most were also the most worried about losing human connection. They could name what was disappearing. They used it anyway."
The Finding
Higher levels of chatbot experience and greater satisfaction with chatbot interactions were positively correlated with increased concerns about the loss of human connection and emotional support.
Al-Zahrani (2025)
Card 4 Disruption
The Epistemic Gap
"AI agents don't just answer questions — they become the place where judgment was supposed to develop. The task gets done. The capacity to evaluate it does not."
The Finding
Student-chatbot exchanges were overwhelmingly cognitive — task questions, concept clarification, solution requests. Metacognitive interactions lacked critical depth: students offloaded reflective thinking to AI rather than doing it themselves or with peers.
Feng (2025); Fan et al. (2024)
Card 5 Design
The Design Alternative
"What if AI never answered a student's question — and instead asked a classmate to?"
The Finding
A conversational agent called Clair analyzed student dialogue to identify the right moment to intervene — then prompted students to build on each other's reasoning and connect contributions to domain concepts. Result: significant increases in productive peer dialogue.
de Araujo et al. (2024)
Card 6 Design
The Role Reversal
"When the AI knew less than the students, the students explained more to each other. When the AI knew more, they stopped talking."
The Finding
When the agent was positioned as a less-knowledgeable peer, student groups more frequently posed questions, tested the agent's understanding, and built on prior exchanges to explain concepts. The expert agent produced dependency; the peer agent produced dialogue.
Nguyen (2023)
Card 7 Limits
The Listening Illusion
"AI listened better than most humans. Participants felt heard, felt free to express themselves, felt connected. But they were just as lonely afterward as when they started. The experience of connection was real. The connection was not."
The Finding
Across three experiments (N = 668), AI listening satisfied needs for autonomy and relatedness. But it did not reduce loneliness — not even for participants highest in trait loneliness. AI created the experience of a supportive relationship without producing the relational benefits that human listening provides.
Weinstein, Itzchakov, & Maniaci (2025)
